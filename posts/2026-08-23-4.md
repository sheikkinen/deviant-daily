# Hands Hold Decades

Weathered hands rest on pale wood, their surface a map of time—deep creases, age spots, and the particular fragility of age rendered in warm amber light. The fingers curve protectively around a fanned arrangement of vintage photographs, sepia-toned and faded, each portrait a face from another era. The light from an unseen window catches the skin with almost sculptural clarity, creating subtle halos along the knuckles and ridges.

Behind this intimate tableau, roses bloom in various states of decay and vitality—burnt orange buds, deep crimson blooms, and darkened petals in soft focus. The flowers occupy the background like silent witnesses, their colors muted by distance and the gentle grain that pervades the entire scene. A glass vessel holds them, catching diffused light, while dust motes drift through the air like suspended time.

The composition speaks of solitude and safekeeping. These hands do not grip or clench; they cradle. The photographs—showing faces from different generations and eras—rest as precious cargo, protected by flesh that has endured decades. The window frame glows pale on the left, suggesting an ordinary room transformed by afternoon light into something reverent, a space where memory dwells.

The scene holds no tears visible on the skin, no explicit sorrow—only the profound quiet of someone who tends to what remains. The vintage quality of the image itself mirrors the vintage of the photographs, collapsing past and present into a single moment of quiet devotion. Be Art. Be Unique.

> These hands remember what the world forgot.
