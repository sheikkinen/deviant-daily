# Scars of Ascension

A figure stands upon scorched stone, wings unfurled across a landscape burning between worlds. The feathers are layered in shadow and amber—not pristine, but carved by purpose. Runic inscriptions trace the primary flight feathers in gold, each mark a question asked, a doubt survived, a moment of becoming. The musculature of his bare shoulders speaks of trials endured rather than battles won.

Behind him stretches a valley caught in dusk-and-fire, where smoke rises like prayer and flame licks at the distant ridges. The sky above holds no single answer—clouds gather in silver and ash, neither heaven's clarity nor hell's darkness, but the liminal space where transformation occurs. The wings themselves seem to hold that same duality: shadow pooling in their depths while light catches their edges like memory.

The figure's downward gaze suggests neither triumph nor despair, but acknowledgment. His posture carries weight that wings alone cannot lift—the weight of regeneration, of scars that have become strength, of questions that have become wisdom. The runes glowing softly against dark plumage are not wounds but badges of authenticity, proof that this ascension was earned through doubt, not granted through innocence.
