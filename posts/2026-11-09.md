# Vigil Beneath the Broken Glasshouse

A skull-faced figure stands in the foreground, her bone-pale face cracked like old porcelain, eyes hollowed and filled with a cold blue glow. A tall crown of banded blue and gold rises from her brow, its gilded ridges catching light the way a reliquary catches candle flame. Dry branches and withered petals spike outward from the crown and drape across her shoulders, as if the conservatory had grown up around her and never let go.

Behind her the glasshouse stretches into darkness, its arched iron ribs holding shards of pale moonlight where panes have shattered. Vines hang from the rafters in loose ropes, and the floor is a shallow sheen of wet glass and fallen leaves. Mist drifts low between the columns, softening the outlines of pale figures in long gowns who turn and sway in a slow, unhurried dance.

Nothing in this hall moves quickly. The blooms have gone brown at their edges, but a few deep plum blossoms still hold their color near the right margin, heavy and unmoved. The light comes from no single source: it spills through broken roofs, rests on the crown's gold, and leaves the figure's throat and collarbone in shadow, so she seems to listen rather than speak.

She has waited here since the last music faded, and the dancers have never once looked toward her. Her crown is still polished, her petals still hold a faint scent of rain, and the glass keeps reflecting a sky no one remembers. Be Art. Be Unique.
