# Tide Where the Llama Keeps Watch

A woman rises from dark teal water in a burst of white spray, her long black hair whipping back in wet ropes as she pushes forward out of the surf. Her skin glistens with droplets, and a lace bralette and lace-trimmed bottoms cling to her body, the pale crochet catching the light like sea foam.

Behind her stands a cream-coated llama, its fleece warm and tawny along the flank, with white lace-like patterns laid over its hide as though the animal itself were part of the same transformation. Its head is held high and still, its dark eye steady, watching the sea as if it has been waiting for her return.

The backdrop is a wall of rust-brown relief panels carved in maze-like geometry, a labyrinth of square channels and interlocking corridors. The panels hold the warm copper light while the water below churns in cold blue-green swirls, and the whole scene sits on a black plinth as if it were a monument in a gallery hall.

Between the stone labyrinth and the restless tide, the figure seems caught at the moment of crossing, half-drowned or half-reborn, her gaze turned sideways and her mouth painted dark red. Be Art. Be Unique.

> "The sea gives back what it takes, and the llama remembers."
