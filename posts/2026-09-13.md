# Apocalypse Mirrored

A wearer of golden-rimmed sunglasses sits motionless, their composure unshaken as twin lenses become windows into catastrophe. Within each circular reflection, a metropolis burns—towering Art Deco spires pierce through roiling smoke and flame, their geometric silhouettes rendered in meticulous detail against an apocalyptic sky. Debris swirls in the inferno, and the city's skeletal remains crumble beneath waves of orange fire and billowing black clouds.

Yet within this urban devastation, an intimate moment unfolds: two figures draw close, their faces nearly touching in a gesture of tenderness amid ruin. They stand amid the wreckage as if the world's ending matters less than the presence of another soul. The contrast is stark—their silhouettes glow softly against the chaos, a small island of connection in an ocean of destruction.

The wearer's face remains eerily serene, skin rendered with photorealistic precision, every pore visible, every shadow deliberate. Their detachment is profound: while apocalypse rages in the glass, they gaze forward with unmoved calm, as though they have already accepted what lies beyond the lenses. The juxtaposition speaks of dissociation, of watching the world end through a barrier of tinted glass.

This artwork captures the paradox of modern existence—the ability to witness catastrophe and remain untouched, to see passion bloom in ruins, to observe everything while belonging to nothing. The sunglasses become both shield and mirror, protecting the wearer from a reality they can only view from behind glass. Be Art. Be Unique.

> The world burns, but we are still here.
