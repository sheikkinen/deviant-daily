# Gilded Sun in the Ravens' Wood

Beneath a pale moon hung low over a tangle of black branches, a golden figure stands in a forest that seems to hold its breath. His skin gleams with a metallic, gilded sheen, and small crimson scratches trace his chest, shoulder, and forearm like the marks of a hard-won night. The light that falls on him is cool and silver, yet he seems to carry a warmth of his own, as though the sun he once was still smoulders beneath the surface.

A torn drape of white silk hangs from one shoulder, pinned with a golden clasp shaped like a coiled ornament. Its edges are ragged, frayed by thorns or by time, and dark holes bloom across the fabric where it has given way. Across the crown of his head sits a band of black leather studded with pointed spikes rather than laurel, a crown that speaks of a choice made in shadow rather than glory.

Two ravens perch on a gnarled limb to the left, and a third watches from the trunk on the right, their feathers glossy as wet ink against the moonlight. They face him with the patience of witnesses. His gaze is turned aside, past the viewer, toward something unseen, and his expression holds a quiet grief that sits uneasily beside his divine stature.

The clearing is not a place of triumph but of threshold: the sun-god stands between the dark woods and the open silver sky, weighing what he has gained against what he has given up. Be Art. Be Unique.
