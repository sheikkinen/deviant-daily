# Kiss Over Still Black Water

Two women meet in a swamp where the water lies flat and dark enough to hold the trees upside down. One leans low over the other, her long black hair loose and tangled by some unseen breeze, while the second floats half-submerged with her shoulders bare above the ripples. Their faces almost touch, and the moment hangs there as if the marsh itself has agreed to wait.

The light is thin and green-tinged, filtering through mossy trunks at the edges of the frame. Lily pads drift on the surface, and a small pale orb glints in the reeds, suggesting a world that keeps its own quiet watch. Ripples spread outward from the bodies in the water, and their reflections break and bend across the dark surface.

A brown-capped sparrow stands on the water's edge, unbothered and sharp-eyed, turning its head toward the viewer as if it has been posted there as witness. Its small presence steadies the scene, a reminder that the swamp keeps living around human intensity, indifferent and patient.
