# Frost Glass and the Last Blooms

Within a conservatory sealed by glass gone white with frost, a skeletal figure sits upon a floor of pale marble. Her crown of roses in peach, cream and deep wine, threaded with curling autumn leaves, has begun to wilt at the edges, and the petals seem to hold the last warmth of a season already gone.

Pale grey mist coils around her in long, slow ribbons, rising from the hem of her gauzy gown and drifting past her shoulders as though the air itself were mourning. Her ribcage shows through the thin fabric, and her hands are folded over her chest in a posture that reads as prayer or as holding something too fragile to release.

Beyond the frosted panes, the garden is only suggested in muted olive and bare brown, blurred by ice ferns that climb the glass in feathered patterns. Dead roses lie scattered across the marble like fallen letters, and a few amber leaves drift among the folds of her robe, marking the slow passage of time.

She keeps her vigil in the cold hush of the glasshouse, where nothing grows anymore and nothing has quite stopped trying. Be Art. Be Unique.

> "The roses forgot their color, but I have not forgotten mine."
