# Lantern of the Hollow Star

A skull rises from the dark like a lantern lit from within, its eye sockets burning amber against bone the color of old ivory. Long strands of yellow light pour from its crown and fall past the shoulders, drifting downward until they dissolve into the black beneath the figure.

Behind the head, a galaxy turns slowly in a spiral of violet, rose, and pale blue, while wisps of slate-blue and crimson nebula curl through the surrounding void. Small stars glitter across the canvas, steady and cold, as though the sky itself is listening.

The body beneath is a sculpted, muscular form dusted with tiny specks of starlight and threaded with faint veins of red and blue. Tattered, leaf-like membranes hang from the shoulders and forearms, curling at their edges as if they were shed from something that once grew, and the bony hands at the bottom of the frame reach outward with fingers half-curled.

Nothing in the scene explains who this figure is or what it wants, and that silence is the point. It stands at the threshold between a dead thing and a living cosmos, neither fully dying nor fully reborn. Be Art. Be Unique.

> "The light was never mine; I only carry it through the hair."
