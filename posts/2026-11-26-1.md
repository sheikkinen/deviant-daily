# Where the Red Moon Rests on Frost

A single crimson disk hangs low over the crest of a pale, fleecy hill, its glow bleeding softly into the gray sky. Nothing else in the scene holds color, so the eye goes straight to it, as if it were the only warm thing left in a world that has forgotten how to bloom.

Above the hill, a gnarled tree stretches its limbs across the frame like a slow, tired arm. Its bark is striped in bold black and white swirls, grain that looks carved by wind over many centuries, and its branches sag toward the grass as though they are listening for something beneath the soil.

The hill itself is a dense mat of silvered tufts, each strand catching the diffuse light of an overcast afternoon. Clouds drift in long, flat bands, and at the far edge the land fades into a hazy horizon, suggesting a valley that is still half-asleep under the weather.

Some say the red disk is not a sun at all but a seed that the tree lets fall once every hundred winters, and the hill grows fur to keep it warm. Be Art. Be Unique.

> "Hold the red light close; the frost remembers everything it touches."
