# Horns in the Fluorescent Maze

In the sterile glow of Aisle 9, the ancient and the mundane collide. A minotaur stands upright in a navy hoodie, his massive horns nearly brushing the drop ceiling, carrying a yellow shopping basket filled with groceries. His dark, weathered face bears the weight of a creature born for labyrinths, not supermarkets—yet here he navigates the fluorescent-lit corridors with the grim determination of one who has learned to exist in both worlds.

Surrounding him, ordinary shoppers move through their routines, some oblivious to his presence, others casting sidelong glances at this impossible figure. A woman in red pushes a cart; children clutch their parents' hands; the crowd ebbs and flows like an indifferent tide. The shelves tower on either side, stacked with snacks and beverages—a modern labyrinth of consumer goods, each aisle a passage, each choice a fork in the path.

His expression is one of deep annoyance, a barely contained frustration at the absurdity of his circumstances. The horns, those ancient symbols of power and transgression, are reduced to obstacles in a space designed for smaller creatures. Yet he persists, basket in hand, a titan reduced to the quotidian struggle of grocery shopping.

In this fluorescent purgatory, myth meets the everyday, and the minotaur—that legendary monster of legend—becomes simply another shopper navigating the crowded aisles. Be Art. Be Unique.
