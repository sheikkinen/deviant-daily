# Requiem in the Abyss

In a deluge that drowns the world, a figure of pure shadow rises from the flooded ruins—a demon-woman crowned with twisted horns, her body wreathed in darkness and dripping water. Her claws rake the storm-heavy air as rain falls like shattered glass, each droplet catching the pale light of a dying sky. The chiaroscuro clings to her form, sculpting muscle and malice from the murk.

Against this apocalyptic stage stands a solitary woman in crimson, her red dress an unbroken flame in the grey tempest. She holds a guitar with the calm of ritual, her wide-brimmed hat shadowing a face turned toward some distant hope. The water reflects her defiance—a mirror of blood and resolve in the flooded city's bones.

Around them, the architecture of a fallen civilization crumbles into the dark river. Pillars and broken stone scatter the composition, framing this impossible confrontation between chaos incarnate and human grace. The rain is relentless, the mood suffocating, yet the guitarist does not flee.

This is the moment where beauty refuses to yield to the abyss, where art persists in the face of damnation. In the shadow of horns and claws, music plays on. Be Art. Be Unique.

> Let her rage—my song will outlast the storm.
