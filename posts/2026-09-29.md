# Harbour's Bleeding Heart

The storm breaks over a Nordic harbour at dusk, rain streaking the air in diagonal lines that blur sky and water into one turbulent expanse. Waves surge against the seawall with visible force, their crests catching fragments of reflected light—amber, orange, and sickly yellow bleeding across the agitated surface like wounds in dark fabric. The waterfront buildings stand resolute behind, their warm windows glowing defiantly against the grey deluge, each light a small defiance of the encroaching night.

The colour reflects not with clarity but with distortion—the harbour lights smear downward through the restless water, fractured and elongated by motion and rain. Reds and golds stretch into streaks that seem to weep into the blackness below, creating an almost expressionist dissolution of boundary between land and sea. The architectural silhouettes of the waterfront—pitched roofs, chimneys, the solitary tower—remain solid anchors, yet their warmth feels isolated, almost melancholic against the violence of the storm.

A city exhales its evening into chaos. The rain continues its relentless percussion, the waves their rhythmic assault, and between them the light persists in its distorted, bleeding reflection—neither fully extinguished nor fully present. This is the hour when urban solitude deepens, when the harbour becomes a mirror for internal weather, and the lights of home seem simultaneously close and infinitely distant.

In this moment of tempest and reflection, the city's soul reveals itself through trembling colour and dark water. The storm speaks not in destruction but in mood—a conversation between the built world and the natural one, between warmth and cold, between the desire to shine and the inevitability of shadow.
