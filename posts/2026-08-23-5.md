# Vigil in Geometry

A figure kneels in prayer within a chamber of pure form—walls reduced to color blocks of dusty rose and slate, the geometry of devotion made visible. Above, a pale orb hangs luminous against deep indigo, neither sun nor moon but something liminal, something watched for. The figure's silhouette is rendered in overlapping planes: burgundy, ash, cream—a body composed of angles rather than curves, yet the upward tilt of the head speaks unmistakably of longing.

The space around her is emptied of ornament. Negative space becomes its own architecture, a void that amplifies rather than diminishes. Gradient shadows pool across the floor in muted grays and blues, suggesting candlelight or the first breath of dawn. The pink rectangle at her feet—an altar, a threshold, a offering made manifest in color alone.

In the economy of line and hue, emotion distills to its essence: the angle of the neck, the reach of an arm, the pull toward the celestial form above. St. Agnes' Eve stripped to its skeleton, where anticipation needs no ornament, only direction. Where faith is rendered as a single gesture held in suspension between earth and sky.

The ritual persists not in what is shown but in what is withheld—the face unseen, the prayer unspoken, the answer suspended in that pale sphere. Here, in this room of blocks and silence, the spiritual finds its truest voice. Be Art. Be Unique.

> I watch the light descend and wait for what is promised.
