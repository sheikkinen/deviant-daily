# Cube-Demons' Carnival

A procession of geometric horrors shambles across a landscape of ash and ember. The demons—each a grotesque fusion of cubic form and organic malice—bear multiple eyes that stare with vacant hunger, their mouths gaping in permanent hunger or silent screams. Spikes erupt from their angular frames like the teeth of some impossible machinery. Behind them, towers of the damned claw at a sky split between sickly cream and blood-rust, while spiraling clouds of smoke coil upward in dizzying loops.

The ground beneath them lies barren and scorched, a gray wasteland scattered with the detritus of torment. Flames lick upward in shades of rust and amber, casting an infernal glow across the scene. The architecture of this hell is all sharp angles and impossible geometries—the cube-demons themselves seem to be both inhabitants and extensions of the landscape's own architecture, as if the very realm has grown teeth and learned to walk.

The style carries the hallucinatory weight of Max Ernst's frottage and grattage techniques—dreamlike, unsettling, with surfaces that seem to shift between smooth and textured, between intention and accident. The rendering oscillates between meticulous line-work and visceral smudging, creating an atmosphere of barely-contained chaos.

In this carnival of the damned, order and disorder dance together in perpetual conflict. The demons gather as if for some infernal congregation, their presence both comic and deeply unsettling. Be Art. Be Unique.
