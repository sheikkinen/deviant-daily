# The Last Vigil

A solitary figure stands motionless in a forest of ash and bone, silhouetted against a pallid moon that bleeds no warmth. Skeletal trees claw at a grey sky, their bark stripped to pale wood, their branches bare of any living leaf. The ground beneath is a graveyard of decay—fallen logs, scattered debris, and the detritus of a world unmade. No bird calls. No wind moves. Only the weight of absolute stillness.

The light that filters through the canopy is funeral, draining colour into monochrome. A shallow stream or path of dark water cuts through the forest floor, reflecting nothing but shadow. The figure's dark coat absorbs what little luminescence remains, becoming part of the landscape's mourning. Every visible surface speaks of entropy: wood crumbles, earth hardens, growth ceases.

The moon hangs like a final witness, neither rising nor setting, suspended in the grey expanse above. The forest forms a cathedral of ruin, its pillars of dead timber creating a tunnel through which nothing returns. The figure's posture suggests neither movement nor hope—only presence, a consciousness bearing witness to the world's slow surrender to the dark.

In this fractured realm, all promise has bled away. The sun, if it still burns, hides behind the blight. Silence is the only law here, and the only response is stillness. Be Art. Be Unique.

> The moon remembers what the earth has forgotten.
