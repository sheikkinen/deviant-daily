# Wind-Torn Hymn in the Alley

A woman stands mid-stride in a narrow brick alley slick with night damp, her head flung back and mouth open as if she has just released a cry into the dark. Her black hair lifts and streams sideways, shot through with deep red strands, carried by a wind that the scene seems to have summoned rather than merely felt.

Her arms reach wide and her fingers spread like claws catching the last of the air. The sleeves of her gown are shredded into pale blue, violet and magenta ribbons of sheer fabric that flutter behind her, while a patterned bodice with a geometric weave holds her close beneath a dark lace choker. The alley walls rise on either side, their windows dark and cracked, and faint green and amber lamplight glints across the wet cobblestones.

Around her, sharp translucent shards of cyan, lime, and hot pink hang in the air like broken glass or fallen geometry, and thin white filaments sweep through the frame in long arcs, as though the street itself is caught in a spiral of motion. Nothing in the scene settles; every edge seems to be in the middle of a turn.
