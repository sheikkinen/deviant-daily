# Tendrils of the Abyss

A face emerges from darkness, skin slick with frost and moisture, eyes wide and luminous with the pale blue glow of something otherworldly. The features are sharp, gaunt, carved by exposure to forces beyond mortal understanding. Moisture beads across the skin like tears that will not fall, while the hair clings wetly to the skull, matted and dark as the void itself.

Around this visage, tendrils writhe—icy black strands that wind and coil with terrible purpose, studded with crystalline points that catch what little light exists. These are not mere appendages; they are the visible manifestation of dread itself, creeping with deliberate slowness, their grip tightening inch by inch. The cold radiates from them in palpable waves, a presence that suffocates rather than consumes.

The atmosphere is one of inevitable surrender. The eyes betray recognition of something already known—that resistance has failed, that the grip will not loosen. There is no struggle visible, only the hollow acceptance of one caught between waking and dissolution, between the world of warmth and the realm of creeping ice. The silence hangs heavy, broken only by the faint whisper of crystalline movement.

In this frozen moment, dread takes form. It is neither swift nor merciful, but patient, inexorable, and utterly certain of its claim. Be Art. Be Unique.

> The cold does not burn—it simply waits.
